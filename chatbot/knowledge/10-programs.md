## Pre-trainings (the four "Basics" programs)

A pre-training is a **7-week online program** for ambitious high-school and
university-freshman students, designed to guide career selection at the
undergraduate level. Every pre-training has two components:

1. **Advanced Curricula** — AP courses and introductory college-level courses
   from Khan Academy.
2. **Expert Sessions** — anecdotal career experience from working
   professionals in that industry.

By the end, a learner is informed about the careers available in their industry
and can plan their studies accordingly. All pre-trainings include AP course
material, plus extra courses that vary by track (for example introduction to
electrical engineering, Python programming, or internet safety).

Who it's for: African youth aged 17–19, in Grade 11, Grade 12, recently
graduated, or university freshmen.

The four tracks are:

### Software Engineering Basics
### Engineering Basics
### Law Basics
### Medicine Basics

All four run in the same 7-week format described above and are applied to
through the same form at **/apply**. They differ in the expert sessions and in
which additional courses are bundled with the AP material.

### Taking two at once — the Duo Pack

Learners can join two cohorts simultaneously. A popular combination, chosen for
effective time management, is **Medicine & Software Engineering Basics**. The
Duo Pack is one of the deposit packages listed on /pay.

## Research Scholars Program

A **16-week, fully online research mentorship program** that takes Ethiopian
high schoolers from zero research experience to a completed, publishable paper.
It is a separate program from the pre-trainings, with its own signup.

- **Duration:** 16 weeks, fully online
- **Tracks:** STEM & Natural Sciences, or Social Sciences & Policy
- **Cohort size:** 15–25 students, organised into 3–5 mentor pods
- **Who it's for:** Grade 10–12 students, roughly ages 15–18
- **Output:** a completed research paper, published for every student who finishes

The 16 weeks run in four phases: Research Foundations (weeks 1–4), Proposal &
Literature Review (5–8), Research, Analysis & Drafting (9–13), and Revision,
Publication & Symposium (14–16). The first four weeks are spent building
research literacy before any paper writing begins. The program closes with a
virtual research symposium where each student presents their paper.

**STEM & Natural Sciences track** covers areas such as public health and
epidemiology, environmental science, nutrition and food systems, AI and
technology in Ethiopia, water and sanitation, agricultural science, climate,
math education, and infectious disease modelling. Typical methods: secondary
data analysis (WHO, World Bank, Ethiopian CSA datasets), literature reviews,
meta-analysis, surveys, and computational modelling.

**Social Sciences & Policy track** covers areas such as education access and
equity, youth unemployment, gender and women's rights, urban planning in Addis
Ababa, the informal economy, digital finance, electoral policy, refugees and
displacement, and mental health stigma. Typical methods: qualitative
interviews, comparative policy analysis, case studies, thematic literature
review, discourse analysis, and basic survey design.

Selection is **merit-based but not hyper-exclusive** — Ethioware looks for
curiosity, commitment, and the capacity to be stretched, not prior research
credentials. There is no minimum GPA cutoff; review is holistic.

**Important:** Research Scholars has its own early-interest signup at
**/research-scholars** — it does *not* use the /apply form. Never send a
Research Scholars enquiry to /apply.
