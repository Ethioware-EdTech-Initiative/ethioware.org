## Mentorship in the pre-trainings

Pre-trainings are built around **expert sessions**: working professionals from
the learner's industry share their own career path — how they got where they
are, what they'd do differently, and what a newcomer should do first. This sits
alongside the AP and introductory college-level coursework.

## Mentorship in the Research Scholars Program

Research Scholars uses a **pod model** rather than pure one-to-one mentorship.
Pods make the program sustainable, reduce dropout (students are accountable to
peers, not only to a mentor), and mirror how real research teams work.

- **Research Mentor** — a PhD student, postdoc or faculty member, ideally of
  Ethiopian diaspora background or with Ethiopia expertise. Leads a pod of 3–4
  students in the same track and guides research questions, methodology and
  interpretation. Commitment: 3 group sessions plus 3 individual 1-on-1s per
  student.
- **Writing Coach** — shared across 2–3 pods, specialising in academic English.
  Runs workshops in weeks 11–12 and is available by appointment for a 30-minute
  individual session, with priority for students who find academic English
  harder. Commitment: 2 group workshops plus 1 optional 1-on-1.
- **Program Coordinator** — Ethioware staff. Runs the Telegram group, sends
  weekly reminders, tracks milestone submissions and keeps everyone on
  schedule. The single point of contact for logistics.

Tools are chosen for free access, low bandwidth and mobile compatibility:
Telegram (primary hub), the Ethioware LMS (weekly PDF templates, tutorials,
milestone tracking), Google Meet or Zoom for sessions (recorded for students
with connectivity problems), Google Scholar and African Journals Online for
search, Zotero for citations, and Google Forms/Sheets for students running
surveys. Every week's core materials are distributed as PDFs so students can
work offline — live sessions are supplementary, not the only path to content.

## Publication pathways (Research Scholars)

Every student who completes the program has their paper published. Mentors help
target the venue:

- **Guaranteed:** the *Ethioware Research Compendium*, Ethioware's own curated
  online publication — every completer is published here, giving each student a
  citable, public URL.
- **Accessible:** the *Journal of Student Research* and *Curieux Academic
  Journal*, peer-reviewed student journals that are free to submit to and open
  access; and *African Journals Online* student sections or Ethiopian
  university conferences (AAU, Jimma, Hawassa).
- **Stretch:** international competitions such as Regeneron ISEF, Scholastica
  or MIT THINK, for outstanding papers.

## Certificates

Learners who complete a program receive a certificate, published at a short,
permanent URL in the form `ethioware.org/<code>` — for example
`ethioware.org/WI10092516`. Visiting that URL directly shows the certificate,
so it can be shared or verified by anyone with the code. Research Scholars
completers also receive a Program Certificate and a Mentor Evaluation Letter at
the closing symposium.

## Volunteering as a mentor

Mentors apply at **/support** (Become a Mentor). A mentor guides a small pod of
students through the heart of their research journey — roughly **4 hours a week
for about a month and a half, mid-cohort**. The signup asks for a LinkedIn
profile and a track preference: STEM & Natural Sciences, Social Sciences &
Policy, or no preference. Ethioware follows up by email within 2–3 days. A
Mentor Commitment Guide describing expectations, schedule and a typical week is
linked from /support — worth reading before applying.

## Interning at Ethioware

There are two internship categories:

- **Technical** — 3 months, for developers, product managers and data analysts.
  Registrations open 2 months before the summer.
- **Creative** — duration varies by role, for graphics designers, video
  editors, UI/UX designers, and youth ambassadors (high-school students only).
  Registrations are open all year round.

For either, write to info@ethioware.org.
